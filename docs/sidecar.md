# Sidecar: your own punches inside the workout

*[Versione italiana](it/sidecar.md)*

The automatic generator decides everything on its own. The **sidecar** turns
that around: you listen to the track, tap out the punches you want where you
want them, and the tool builds the workout around yours.

## Marking

Press play and tap. Each tap leaves a **marker**, an instant in time. The
marker block shows the waveform zoomed in with the beat grid underneath, so
you can see whether you are landing on the beat or beside it.

Two aids, both switchable:

- **Imprecision correction** — the marker snaps to the nearest onset in the
  audio, if there is one. Nobody taps perfectly.
- **Magnetic grid snap** — the marker aligns to the nearest beat subdivision,
  within a fraction of a beat. Without it your punches sound "dirty" next to
  the generated ones, and it is a difference you hear immediately.

There is also a **minimum gap between two punches**: below that distance the
second is discarded, because it is not physically throwable. The panel tells
you how many were left out.

## The three modes

### Harmonise

Your punches and the generated ones coexist. The tool keeps generating its
choreography across the whole track, and where you marked, your punch wins the
conflict.

Useful when you want to *add* accents to a workout you are already happy with.

### Markers only

Nothing but your punches, plus automatic obstacles (squats and dodges, which
are not markable). The rest of the track stays silent.

Useful when you want total control and are willing to mark everything.

### Extend

*"I give you the first minute and a half of three, the tool handles the rest."*

This is the most ambitious mode, and the one that took the most rounds of
correction. It now works like this: **it does not just look at where you
marked, but how**.

Markers are grouped, and each group is classified:

| zone | treatment |
|---|---|
| **rhythmic block** — 4 or more punches close together | you are dictating a rhythm: there the tool adds nothing of its own |
| **isolated accent** — shorter groups | you are setting a landmark: there the automatic layer keeps running, and your punch wins at that instant |
| **everything else** | generated by the tool, imitating the cadence and rhythm of your blocks |

All three treatments coexist within the same track.

#### Why the distinction matters

Previously, "the marked stretch" was defined as the span from the first marker
to the last. That model degenerated predictably: **marking the start and the
end of the song** was enough for the span to cover everything, leaving nothing
to extend, and the mode became identical to "markers only" — silence in
between included.

Telling rhythm from accent solves that case without adding a branch of code:
accents, not being inside any block, receive the automatic layer **by
construction**.

#### The break threshold is yours, not ours

When you are considered to have *stopped* marking is not a fixed number of
seconds: it is **six times your own typical interval** (with a floor of 2 and
a ceiling of 8 bars). Someone throwing sixteenth-note bursts and someone
marking one punch per bar have two different ideas of what a "pause" is, and a
constant would have agreed with only one of them.

#### What it learns, and what it cannot

From your blocks it takes two things:

1. **Cadence** — how many punches per minute you throw.
2. **Rhythmic signature** — where they fall inside the beat: on the beat, off
   the beat, on sixteenths.

The second is the one you hear. Two stretches with the same punches per minute
sound worlds apart if one sits on the beat and the other off it.

**What it cannot learn**: which punch. A marker only says *when*. Move type,
hand and height are still decided by the engine. Extend reproduces your
**timing**, never your combinations.

#### The 45-second threshold

To imitate you it needs at least 45 seconds of rhythmic blocks — summed, not
"first marker to last". From less you do not learn a way of punching: you
photograph four punches.

Below that threshold the rest of the track is generated normally. That is not
an error, and it is declared: the line under the mode selector says what the
tool saw, and a **coloured band** on the marker track shows it — purple for
rhythmic blocks, amber for isolated accents, nothing where the tool generates.

That visible part is not decoration. With the same markers the mode can behave
in two different ways depending on how they are distributed: without showing
it, you would get different results without understanding why, and it would
look like your fault.

## Still to be tuned

Three numbers in the classification are declared choices, not measurements:
how many punches minimum make a block (4), how long it must last (1 bar), and
the break-pause multiplier (6×). A "human marker" does not exist in the game's
official workouts, so there is nothing to derive them from statistically: they
have to be tuned by testing in the headset.
